# Writing guide

Write as if you are explaining the work to a smart person who was not on the project.

## Do

- Start with the result or decision.
- Use short sentences and common words.
- Explain a technical term the first time it appears.
- Use headings and lists to make long pages easy to scan.
- Say “I” for your own work.
- Say who “we” means when describing team work.
- Keep first person when it makes ownership clear. Do not replace it with
  passive language just to avoid repeating “I”.
- Be direct about limits, mistakes, and uncertainty.
- Use exact dates and numbers when they matter.
- Make link text describe where the link goes.
- Put the plain-language result first. Add technical detail after it.
- Make each page useful to someone who reads only the heading and first paragraph.
- Let the evidence carry the claim. Do not add a sentence that tells the reader
  what the evidence should make them think about you.

## Avoid

- claims such as “world-class” or “best-in-class”;
- long résumé-style lists;
- skill meters and walls of technology logos;
- vague praise with no source;
- repeated capability lists;
- taking credit for a team's work;
- jokes or clever headings that hide the point;
- copy that only makes sense with an image or animation.

## Cut before publishing

Delete a sentence when:

- the next sentence proves the same point;
- the same example already appears elsewhere on the page;
- it announces that you are capable, responsible, or thoughtful instead of
  showing the work;
- it exists only to make the paragraph sound finished.

## Final read

Ask:

1. What is the main point?
2. What proves it?
3. Is my role clear?
4. Can someone outside the project understand it?
5. Did I repeat the same evidence?
6. Can I say it with fewer words?
